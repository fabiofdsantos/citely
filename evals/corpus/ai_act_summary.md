# EU AI Act — Summary of Key Provisions

This file is a plain-language summary written for evaluation purposes. It is not
the legal text of the Regulation, and it is deliberately small and stable so
that golden expectations stay valid. The production corpus is configured
separately through `CITELY_CORPUS_PATH`.

## Scope and purpose

The Regulation establishes harmonised rules for placing artificial intelligence
systems on the market in the European Union. It follows a risk-based approach,
imposing obligations in proportion to the risk a system presents.

## Prohibited practices

Article 5 sets out prohibited practices. These include social scoring of natural
persons by public authorities, exploitation of vulnerabilities of specific
groups, and untargeted scraping of facial images to build recognition databases.
Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law
enforcement is prohibited except in narrowly defined circumstances.

## High-risk systems

Systems used in employment, education, essential services, and critical
infrastructure are classified as high-risk. High-risk systems must undergo a
conformity assessment before being placed on the market. Providers of high-risk
systems must establish a risk management system that runs across the entire
lifecycle of the system.

Providers of high-risk systems must also maintain technical documentation, keep
automatically generated logs, and ensure an appropriate level of human oversight.

## Transparency obligations

Providers must ensure that people are informed when they are interacting with an
AI system, unless this is obvious from the context. Synthetic audio, image, and
video content must be marked in a machine-readable format as artificially
generated.

## Governance and penalties

Each Member State designates a national competent authority to supervise
application of the Regulation. Non-compliance with the prohibited practices in
Article 5 attracts the highest tier of administrative fines.
